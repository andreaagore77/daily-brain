Two independent searches today failed to corroborate the **2026-09-24** deadline
on both BJA Second Chance Act tracks. Search surfaced only the FY25 cycle
(closed 2026-05-11) and FY24. That date comes solely from the Instrumentl export
supplied yesterday.

This is probably not a contradiction — Instrumentl tracks live opportunities,
while search results skew heavily toward archived agency pages. But it is now

exactly one source. **Confirm it directly at bja.ojp.gov or grants.gov before
committing writing time**, and before treating Sept 24 as the registration
deadline.

A related eligibility signal worth checking: federal reentry funders typically
expect nonprofit applicants to show **2–3 years of operational experience in
justice-related or workforce programming**. Worth confirming the org clears that
bar before investing in a solo federal application.

## New to the watch list

Neither has a confirmed FY2026 cycle, so neither is actionable today. Both are
strong enough fits to track.

**SAMHSA Adult Reentry Program — up to $400,000/year**
The program purpose reads almost as a description of this organization: expand
SUD treatment and related recovery and reentry services to sentenced adults with
SUD and/or co-occurring disorders returning to family and community after
incarceration. Private nonprofits explicitly eligible. The $400k ceiling is a far
better fit against a $300k budget than the $1M Second Chance Act tracks — closer
to a realistic first federal award than anything currently in tier 1. Last
confirmed announcement is TI-23-006 (pre-2025); no FY2026 cycle surfaced.

**BJA Improving Adult and Youth Crisis Stabilization and Community Reentry**
Funds clinical services and evidence-based activities for reentry and for the
treatment/recovery needs of justice-involved people with mental health, SUD, or
co-occurring disorders. Nonprofits eligible with *or without* 501(c)(3) status.

The notable detail: eligibility **requires** nonprofits to apply in partnership
with the relevant criminal or juvenile justice agency. That is not a barrier
here — it is the org's existing advantage, and it independently corroborates the
partner-led approach already recommended for the Second Chance Act tracks. Only
FY23 and FY25 cycles surfaced.

## Addendum — screening review (same day)

Three previously screened-out items were re-examined at the org's request.
**No items restored.** Two cuts confirmed correct; one moved to undetermined.

**Affordable Housing and Supportive Services Demonstration — cut stands.**
Eligible applicants are Community Action Agencies and tribes funded *directly*
by CSBG in FY2025 **that own affordable housing units**. Clear Conscience
Consulting is neither a CSBG-funded CAA nor a housing owner — two independent
disqualifiers. This system's flag calling that cut "a probable miss" was wrong;
the original screen was right, though the reason given ("housing-provider
focus") understated it. Not a next-year watch item either.

**Strengthening Services to Prevent the Infectious Disease Consequences of Drug
Use — cut stands, reason corrected.** Closest match is CDC's Strengthening
Syringe Services Programs cooperative agreement; prior awards went to RTI
International and NASTAD, and applicants must be able to *award funding to*
syringe services programs and collect performance data across them. That is a
funder-of-funders role, plus a service line the org doesn't run. It had been
filed under "research institution required" — the accurate reason is national
intermediary scale.

**Improving Linkage to Care for People with OUD (U01) — undetermined.**
Eligibility could not be established either way. CDC's OD2A linkage-to-care
work runs through state and local health departments, and U01 is a research
cooperative agreement mechanism — both point away from a small community
nonprofit, but neither was confirmed for this NOFO. It should not have been
filed confidently under "research institution required." Deadline is
2026-12-01, so there is time to read the Eligible Applicants section directly.
