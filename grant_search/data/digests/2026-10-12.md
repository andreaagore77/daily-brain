# Grant digest — 2026-10-12

Last week's opioid settlement lead turned out to be the most substantial finding
of this project. Three county programs identified and added to the pipeline.

## Somerset County micro-grants — the best structural fit found anywhere

**501(c)(3) nonprofits only. One-county applicant pool. Application is a
statement of interest, project objectives, a budget, and proof of tax-exempt
status.** Funded by Somerset County's opioid settlement share: **at least
$450,000 every year for the next 18 years.** A recent round awarded roughly
$50,000 across several nonprofits, so individual awards look like $5,000–$15,000.

This inverts every problem that sank the September items:

| What went wrong in September | What this does instead |
|---|---|
| National applicant pools | One county |
| $1M awards against a $300k budget | Award sized to the organization |
| Full federal application packages | Statement of interest plus a budget |
| Government-only clauses, Category ambiguity, treatment-provider questions | 501(c)(3) nonprofits, full stop |
| One missed cycle = one lost year | Recurs annually for 18 years |

Somerset is a priority county. Entered at **40%**, which puts it third on the
whole board.

## Union County — ~$980,000 a year, for eighteen years

Union holds roughly **$17.7 million over 18 years**, administered by its Office
on Behavioral Health and Substance Misuse with an Opioid Task Force
recommending allocations. RFA 2026-059 sought **recovery support, treatment,
early intervention and harm reduction** — the organization's core work almost
verbatim — and closed 2026-03-18, so cycles appear to run late winter.

Comparable rounds ran up to about $50,000 per agency. Union is a priority
county. Entered at **30%**.

Watch `ucnj.org/dhs/obhsm/opioid-settlement-funds-osf/` and contact OBHSM
before the next RFA — a conversation months ahead of a local RFA is worth more
than a polished application submitted cold.

## Morris County — smaller, adjacent

Publishes opioid settlement priorities with a $5,000 minimum request. Outside
the four priority counties, but inside HFNJ's Greater MetroWest footprint, so
already adjacent to the stated reach. Entered at **20%**.

## Two gaps

**Essex** — no county program surfaced. The state separately allocated $10.5M
over three years for housing and wraparound services for medically complex
individuals in Essex, but that is state-administered. Worth checking Essex
County Health/Human Services directly.

**Jersey City** runs a 2026 cycle but requires applicants to be members of the
Partnership for a HealthierJC — a membership step, not a bar.

## The timing is the good news

Most county 2026 cycles have already closed. Cycles appear to run late winter
into early spring, which means **the next ones are ahead of the organization
rather than behind it** — the first time that has been true since this pipeline
was built. September's five expirations all involved deadlines that had passed
or were passing before anything could start. These have months of runway.
