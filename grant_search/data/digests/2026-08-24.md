## SNAP STEPS may not be the kind of money it looks like

**Programmatic fit is confirmed good.** SNAP Employment & Training is used
nationally to support reentry: work experience, job training, and support
services like transportation and tools for people returning from incarceration.
NJDOL is seeking statewide partnerships. The justice-involved population is
squarely in scope.

**But the financing model may be disqualifying.** The federal SNAP E&T
third-party partner structure is **50/50**: the partner invests its *own
non-federal funds* to deliver services and is reimbursed **up to 50 cents per
dollar spent**.

If New Jersey passes that structure through, SNAP STEPS is not a grant that
supplies operating money. It requires fronting 100% of costs and recovering
half. For a $300,000 organization, a large commitment under that model could be
financially dangerous rather than helpful — the opposite of what a small
nonprofit needs.

This has to be confirmed before anything else, because it may also apply to
**Pathways to Recovery** and **Job Opportunities for Building Success** — both
from the same department, both carrying $1M ceilings, and both currently sitting
high in the pipeline on the assumption that the ceiling represents available
funding. If NJDOL's model is 50/50 across the board, three pipeline items need
re-rating at once.
