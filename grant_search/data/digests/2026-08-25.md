# Grant digest — 2026-08-25

Yesterday's SNAP finding opened a question about how NJDOL pays. Today answers
it, and the answer touches four pipeline items at once.

## All NJDOL grants are cost-reimbursement

Confirmed: NJDOL distributes grant funds **on a reimbursable basis only**.
Grantees are paid for expenditures already made, claimed through the IGX system.
No indication of advance payment anywhere in the guidance.

That applies to four items: **Pathways to Recovery**, **Pathways Round 2 NGO**,
**Job Opportunities for Building Success** (all up to $1M), and **SNAP STEPS**.

For a $300,000 organization this is the practical consequence: winning even a
fraction of a $1M NJDOL award means funding payroll and services out of its own
resources first and recovering the money later. It does not disqualify anyone.
It does mean **working capital is a precondition for accepting an award here,
not an afterthought** — a line of credit, reserves, or a bridge funder needs to
exist before saying yes.

**On the 50/50 question from yesterday, a partial correction.** Some NJDOL
programs do carry an explicit 50/50 match — NJDOL up to 50%, grantee minimum
50%, with employer-paid training wages counting toward the grantee share. But
that was observed on training-type grants, and it is *not* established for
Pathways to Recovery or SNAP STEPS. Yesterday's alarm came from the federal
SNAP E&T third-party model; today shows NJDOL does use 50/50 somewhere, which
makes the question more live, not settled. The NGO PDFs settle it.

## Pathways to Recovery — eligibility confirmed, and a competitor named

Eligible applicants are NJ nonprofits, for-profits, government entities, and
higher ed **with connections to mental health and opioid use disorder treatment
centers**. The organization's provider partnerships satisfy that criterion
directly — this is one of the cleaner eligibility matches in the pipeline.

Program detail: $5M total, up to $1M per grantee over 24 months, covering
training, career services, employment, and supportive services including
transportation, childcare, and clothing.

Worth knowing about the competition: **Integrity House** — a large, established
New Jersey nonprofit — was announced on 2026-07-31 as a $1M Pathways awardee.
That indicates both the calibre of organization winning these and that the
current cycle is already awarded, which is consistent with the 2027 LOI dates
referring to the next round.

## Dropping this routine to weekly

Six consecutive runs have each turned up something material, so the work has
been worth doing. But every one has also ended the same way: nothing approved,
no folders open, 106 placeholders empty, and every remaining question needing a
funder's own page or a fact only the organization holds.

Search has now been taken about as far as it goes. Continuing to fire daily
would mostly generate notifications that repeat themselves, so this routine is
moving to **Mondays at 08:07 UTC**. Nothing else changes — same steps, same
approval gates — and it takes one sentence to put it back to daily.

The three checks that would unblock the most, in order: the Second Chance Act
dates at bja.ojp.gov, the Empower New Jersey scope at cfnj.org, and the NJDOL
NGO PDFs for the reimbursement structure.
