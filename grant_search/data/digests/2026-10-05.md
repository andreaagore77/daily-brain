## New lead: NJ opioid settlement funds

A funding stream this pipeline missed entirely. New Jersey's share of the
national opioid settlements is distributed partly through **counties and
municipalities**, which run their own grant programs — Jersey City publishes one
at `healthierjc.com/grants`, and Essex, Bergen, Union and Somerset are very
likely to run equivalents.

This may be a better-matched stream than anything currently in the pipeline:

- **Directly on mission.** The funds are earmarked for prevention, treatment,
  recovery support and harm reduction for opioid-affected populations.
- **Locally competitive.** County programs draw county-sized applicant pools,
  not national ones — the single biggest lever on win probability, as the
  ranking work established back in August.
- **Awards sized for community organizations**, not $1M federal ceilings
  requiring three times the annual budget and a cash-flow bridge.
- **Administered by people who can be met in person**, and by county structures
  the organization already touches through the courts and IDRC.

Worth checking each priority county's opioid settlement advisory council or
health department. Start with Essex and Union.

## A gap in the source data worth knowing

Three further BJA FY26 tracks surfaced today that were never in the pipeline,
all now passed or passing: **Public Safety and Mental Health Initiative**
(closed 2026-09-25, ~13 awards, explicitly pre-arrest through reentry),
**Comprehensive Opioid, Stimulant and Substance Use Site-Based** (closed
2026-09-24), and **Crisis Response Training** (Grants.gov closes today).

All three belonged to the same DOJ package as the two Second Chance Act tracks.
**The Instrumentl export carried two of five.** For the FY27 cycle, the BJA
solicitation list should be checked directly rather than trusting a matching
service to surface every track — the Public Safety and Mental Health Initiative
in particular reads as a strong fit that never got evaluated.
