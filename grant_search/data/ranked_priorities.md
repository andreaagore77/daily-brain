# Ranked by likelihood of award — 2026-08-19

## What this ranking is, and isn't

These are **judgment estimates, not calibrated statistics.** No public dataset
gives per-program win rates for an organization of this profile, and any number
claiming otherwise is invented. What follows is a defensible ordering built from
seven factors that demonstrably drive grant outcomes:

1. **Eligibility certainty** — is the org clearly an eligible applicant, or is
   that an open question?
2. **Geographic restriction** — a funder limited to Essex/Union/Somerset is
   competing against dozens of applicants, not thousands. This is the single
   biggest lever on the list.
3. **Award-to-budget ratio** — requesting 3x annual budget triggers capacity
   scrutiny. Requesting 5–20% does not.
4. **Competitive pool** — local foundation vs. national federal competition.
5. **Track-record requirements** — prior federal awards, CPA audit, outcome
   data systems, years in operation.
6. **Application burden** — what it costs in staff time to produce a
   competitive submission.
7. **Mission fit** — how little translation the funder has to do to see it.

The org's profile — ~$300k budget, NJ-based, strong court/IDRC partnerships,
founder with lived experience and clinical credentials — scores *high* on
mission fit and local relationships, and *low* on the institutional-scale
signals federal reviewers weight.

## The core finding: probability runs inverse to award size

The most winnable money here is the smallest money. The largest awards on the
list are the least likely by roughly an order of magnitude. Any strategy that
chases the $1M figures first will likely produce zero dollars and consume the
fall.

## Two things the ranking alone doesn't show

**Expected value still favors the big ones.** A 15% shot at $500k is worth more
in expectation than a 50% shot at $2,500. If the goal is maximizing dollars,
Pathways to Recovery (#10) is the single highest-EV item here. The ranking is
about *likelihood*, and likelihood is not the same as where the money is.

**Federal awards can be cash-flow dangerous at this scale.** Most federal
grants reimburse after spending. A $300k-budget organization that wins $1M on
a cost-reimbursement basis must front payroll for months before the first
draw. Winning #15 or #16 without a line of credit or bridge funding in place
could create a worse problem than losing them.

1. **Now through September:** submit Tier A (#1–#4 are days of work, not weeks)
   and CFNJ Empower NJ (#7). This puts real wins on the board fast.
2. **Fall:** HFNJ (#6) by Nov 19, having first resolved the affiliation question.
3. **September 24 decision:** pursue the Second Chance Act tracks *as a partner
   or sub-awardee* under a larger lead applicant rather than solo. Same mission
   value, dramatically better odds, no cash-flow exposure.
4. **Winter into 2027:** the LOI cycle (#10, #13, #14) — with two or three
   2026 wins cited as track record, these move up a tier.

The compounding matters more than any single application: funders ask who else
has funded you, and Tier A answers that question cheaply.
